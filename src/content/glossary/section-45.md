---
term: "Section 45 (Incontestability Rule)"
category: "Legal & Regulation"
plainEnglishSummary: "A statutory shield under Indian insurance law that bars an insurance company from calling a life insurance policy into question or rejecting a death claim on any ground whatsoever after three continuous years from policy issuance or revival."
legalOrRegulatoryBasis: "Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938 (As amended by Insurance Laws Amendment Act, 2015)."
commonTrapOrConfusion: "The 3-year clock resets upon policy revival. If a policy lapses and is subsequently revived with a fresh health declaration, the 3-year contestability period begins anew from the date of revival."
relatedTerms: ["Early Claim", "Repudiation", "Utmost Good Faith"]
---

Section 45 is the strongest consumer protection clause in Indian insurance jurisprudence. Prior to 2015, insurers could contest claims indefinitely alleging historical non-disclosures. The 2015 amendment created an absolute statutory bar after 36 months, placing the full burden of medical and financial underwriting firmly upon the insurer prior to policy issuance.
