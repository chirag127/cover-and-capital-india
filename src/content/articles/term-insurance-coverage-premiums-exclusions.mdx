## 1. What is Pure Term Life Insurance?
Pure term life insurance is the only insurance contract that strictly decouples **protection** from **investment accumulation**. You pay an annual, half-yearly, or monthly premium. In exchange, the insurer guarantees that if you die during the agreed policy term (e.g. between age 28 and 65), the company will pay an agreed capital sum (e.g. ₹1.5 Crore) directly to your designated nominees.

If you survive to the end of the policy term, the contract terminates. There is no maturity bonus, survival payout, or cash surrender value.

$$\text{Premium (Net)} = \text{Sum Assured} \times q_x \times v + \text{Expenses Loading}$$

Where:
* $q_x$ is the statistical probability of a life aged $x$ dying during the policy year (derived from the Indian Assured Lives Mortality Table IALM 2012-14 Ult).
* $v = \frac{1}{1 + i}$ is the present-value discount factor based on the insurer's reserve yield.

## 2. Regular Pay vs Limited Pay: The Present Value Reality
A major marketing trend among Indian insurance brokers is promoting **Limited Pay** structures (e.g. "Pay for 5 Years and Get Covered till Age 60" or "Pay till 60 and Get Covered till Age 85").

Let us evaluate the mathematics for a 30-year-old non-smoker purchasing ₹1 Crore cover up to age 65 (35-year term):

| Payment Structure | Annual Premium (Excl. GST) | Total Years Paid | Total Nominal Outflow | Present Value of Outflow @ 7% Discount Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular Pay (35 Years)** | ₹12,500 | 35 | ₹4,37,500 | **₹1,62,400** |
| **Limited Pay (10 Years)** | ₹29,800 | 10 | ₹2,98,000 | **₹2,08,600** |
| **Limited Pay (5 Years)** | ₹52,400 | 5 | ₹2,62,000 | **₹2,14,800** |

### The Mathematical Finding
Although Limited Pay requires a smaller *nominal* total outflow (₹2.98 Lakh vs ₹4.37 Lakh), the **Present Value (PV) of your payments is significantly higher** under Limited Pay (₹2,08,600 vs ₹1,62,400). 

By paying upfront in your 30s, you hand over valuable capital that could have compounded in your personal investment portfolio at 10%–12% for decades. Regular pay allows you to pay future premiums using depreciated, inflation-eroded rupees.

> **When Limited Pay Makes Sense**: Only for individuals with highly cyclical or finite earning horizons (e.g., commercial airline pilots with early retirement ages, freelance consultants, or merchant navy officers). Salaried employees with stable corporate careers should generally choose **Regular Pay**.

## 3. The Fallacy of "Return of Premium" (TROP)
Insurers heavily market "Term with Return of Premium" (TROP) with psychological slogans like *"Get 100% of Your Money Back If Nothing Happens!"*

Let us analyze the cash flows:
* **Pure Term Plan**: ₹13,000 per year for 30 years. Cover = ₹1 Crore. Total paid = ₹3,90,000. Maturity = ₹0.
* **TROP Plan**: ₹32,000 per year for 30 years. Cover = ₹1 Crore. Total paid = ₹9,60,000. Maturity = ₹9,60,000 returned.

### The Opportunity Cost Breakdown
The difference in premium is $\Delta = \text{INR } 32,000 - \text{INR } 13,000 = \text{INR } 19,000$ every year.
If you simply took the pure term plan and invested that ₹19,000/year into a conservative instrument yielding **7.1% (such as PPF)**:
* In 30 years, that ₹19,000 annual investment grows to **₹19.2 Lakhs**.
* Under TROP, the insurer gives you back only **₹9.6 Lakhs**.
* You surrender **₹9.6 Lakhs of real wealth** to the insurance company just for the psychological comfort of getting your nominal money back.

## 4. Underwriting Disclosures & Medical Realities

### Tobacco & Smoking Status
In Indian life insurance underwriting, anyone who has consumed cigarettes, bidis, cigars, chewing tobacco (gutkha, khaini), hookah, or nicotine vapes in the **last 36 months** is classified as a smoker.
* Smoker premium rates are typically **40% to 70% higher** than non-smoker rates due to higher actuarial mortality.
* *Do not hide smoking*: If an applicant checks "Non-Smoker" to save ₹6,000/year, and dies of lung cancer or cardiac arrest within the first 3 years, the forensic investigator will request medical hospital records. Any mention of smoking history in physician notes triggers immediate claim repudiation under Section 45.

### Mandatory Medical Tests
For sums assured of ₹1 Crore and above, Indian insurers typically require:
1. Complete Blood Count (CBC) & ESR.
2. Fasting Blood Sugar & HbA1c (for diabetes).
3. Lipid Profile (Cholesterol, Triglycerides).
4. Liver Function Tests (LFT) & Kidney Function Tests (KFT / Serum Creatinine).
5. Resting Electrocardiogram (ECG) and Treadmill Test (TMT) for applicants above age 40.
6. Cotinine Urine Test (to detect nicotine metabolites).

## 5. Contractual Exclusions: Reading the Fine Print
Unlike health insurance, pure term life insurance has virtually **zero exclusions** for natural or accidental death, with two specific exceptions:

### 1. The 12-Month Suicide Exclusion
Under standardized IRDAI life policy wordings:
* If the life assured commits suicide within **12 months from the date of commencement of risk**, the claim is void. Nominees receive only 80% to 100% of premiums paid.
* If the policy was lapsed and revived, the 12-month exclusion clock **resets from the date of revival**.
* After 12 continuous months of an active policy, death by suicide is fully covered.

### 2. Participation in Criminal or Unlawful Acts
If the life assured dies as an active participant in an illegal, criminal, or felonious enterprise, the contract relieves the insurer of the death benefit liability.
