## 1. The Statutory Landscape: Enactment of the Income-tax Act, 2025
Effective **April 1, 2026** (Financial Year 2026–27 / Assessment Year 2026–27), the **Income-tax Act, 2025** came into force in India, replacing the six-decade-old Income-tax Act, 1961.

While the new statute reorganizes direct tax law into 23 chapters and 16 schedules, it preserves structural continuity for life insurance taxation through **Schedule II, Clause 2 (Schedule II(2))**, which supersedes the historical Section 10(10D).

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    Legacy[Income-tax Act, 1961] -->|Repealed 1 April 2026| NewAct[Income-tax Act, 2025]
    Legacy --> S10D[Section 10(10D) Exemption]
    Legacy --> S56[Section 56(2)(xiii) High Premium Tax]
    S10D --> Recod1[Recodified: Schedule II, Clause 2]
    S56 --> Recod2[Recodified: Schedule of Income from Other Sources]
```

## 2. The 3 Statutory Pillars of Tax Exemption

### Pillar 1: Universal Exemption on Death Benefits
Under Schedule II, Clause 2, any sum received upon the **death of the life assured** is **100% tax-free** in the hands of the nominee:
* Applies across **all** policy types (pure term, return of premium, ULIPs, traditional endowments, money-back).
* Applies regardless of premium amount (even if annual premium is ₹50 Lakh).
* Applies regardless of the premium-to-sum-assured ratio.
* *Sole Exception*: Employer Keyman Insurance policies where the company receives proceeds.

### Pillar 2: The 10% Premium-to-Sum-Assured Ratio Test
To qualify for tax-free maturity or survival benefits, the policy must maintain a minimum life protection cover:
* **Policies issued on or after 1 April 2012**: Annual premium payable in any year must NOT exceed **10% of the minimum capital sum assured** (i.e. minimum cover must be at least **10 times** the annual premium).
* **Policies issued between 1 April 2003 and 31 March 2012**: Annual premium must not exceed **20% of sum assured** (minimum cover at least 5x premium).
* *The Penalty*: If this test is breached in any single policy year, the entire maturity amount is taxable as income.

### Pillar 3: Statutory Premium Caps on High-Value Policies
Over the past five years, Parliament closed the loophole of using insurance as a tax-free fixed-income haven for High-Net-Worth Individuals (HNIs):

| Policy Category | Governing Issue Date | Statutory Threshold | Tax Treatment If Threshold Exceeded |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ULIPs** | Issued on or after **1 Feb 2021** | Aggregate Annual Premium $\le$ **₹2,50,000** | Taxed as **Capital Gains** under equity/debt fund equivalents. |
| **Traditional Non-ULIPs** | Issued on or after **1 April 2023** | Aggregate Annual Premium $\le$ **₹5,00,000** | Taxed as **Income from Other Sources** at normal slab rates. |
| **Pure Term Insurance** | Any date | **No Limit** | **100% Tax-Free** on death benefit. |

## 3. How Tax on High-Premium Policies Is Computed
If you hold non-ULIP traditional policies issued on or after 1 April 2023 with aggregate annual premiums exceeding ₹5,00,000, how is the tax calculated?

Under **CBDT Circular No. 15 of 2023**, the taxable income is computed using the **Net Profit Method**:

$$\text{Taxable Income} = \text{Maturity / Surrender Payout Received} - \sum_{t=1}^{N} \text{Total Premiums Paid}$$

### Worked Example:
Assume an investor purchases two traditional guaranteed endowment policies in FY 2023–24:
* **Policy A**: Annual Premium = ₹3,00,000 (Sum Assured = ₹30 Lakh).
* **Policy B**: Annual Premium = ₹4,00,000 (Sum Assured = ₹40 Lakh).
* Total aggregate annual premium = **₹7,00,000** (Exceeds ₹5 Lakh limit).

Under CBDT guidelines:
1. The policyholder can elect to claim **100% tax exemption on Policy A** (since ₹3,00,000 $\le$ ₹5,00,000).
2. **Policy B becomes taxable**.
3. If Policy B pays a maturity corpus of ₹65 Lakh after 10 years (total premiums paid = ₹40 Lakh):
$$\text{Taxable Income on Policy B} = \text{INR } 65,00,000 - \text{INR } 40,00,000 = \mathbf{\text{INR } 25,00,000}$$
4. This ₹25,00,000 is added to your total income in the year of receipt and taxed at your applicable slab rate (e.g. 30% slab = ₹7.5 Lakh tax + cess).

## 4. Old vs New Tax Regime: The End of 80C-Driven Selling
For decades, Indian insurance agents closed sales in February and March with the slogan *"Save Tax under Section 80C!"*

Under the **Income-tax Act, 2025**, the default tax regime for all individual taxpayers is the simplified **New Tax Regime**:
* Section 80C deductions (up to ₹1.5 Lakh) are **NOT available** under the default regime.
* Buying an endowment or ULIP policy provides **zero tax deduction** against your salary or business income.
* Unless an individual explicitly opts for the old regime (which is disadvantageous for most salaried employees due to higher tax slab rates), buying insurance for tax saving is an obsolete financial strategy.
