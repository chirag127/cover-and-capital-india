# Indian Life Insurance Taxation: Statutory Framework & Research Guide

## 1. Statutory Context & Transition (FY 2026–27)

### Enactment of the Income-tax Act, 2025
* **Effective Date**: April 1, 2026 (applicable for Assessment Year 2026–27 onwards / Financial Year 2026–27).
* **Repeal and Transition**: The Income-tax Act, 1961 is repealed effective April 1, 2026, subject to transitional provisions saving accrued rights, ongoing assessments, and historical tax characterizations.
* **Recodification Mapping**:
  * **Maturity & Death Benefit Exemption**: The former **Section 10(10D)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961 is recodified under **Schedule II, Clause 2 (Schedule II(2))** of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
  * **Taxation of High-Value Traditional Policies**: Former **Section 56(2)(xiii)** (taxation of income from non-ULIP life insurance policies where aggregate annual premium exceeds ₹5,00,000) is recodified under the unified Schedule for Income from Other Sources.
  * **ULIP Capital Gains**: High-premium ULIPs exceeding ₹2,50,000 (formerly governed by the Fourth and Fifth Provisos to Section 10(10D) and Section 112A/111A) are treated as specified capital assets taxed under the capital gains framework.

## 2. Core Exemption Rules (Schedule II(2) / Former Section 10(10D))

### Rule A: Universal Exemption on Death Benefits
* **Statutory Principle**: Any sum received upon the death of the life assured is **100% tax-exempt in the hands of the nominee or legal heir**.
* **Exceptions**: Applies universally across all policy types (pure term, return of premium, ULIP, traditional endowment, money-back), regardless of premium amount or premium-to-sum-assured ratio, with the sole exception of employer-sponsored Keyman Insurance.

### Rule B: Premium-to-Sum-Assured Ratio Test
* **Policies issued on or after April 1, 2012**: Annual premium payable must NOT exceed **10% of the actual capital sum assured**. (For individuals with severe disability or specified diseases under former Section 80U/80DDB, the cap is 15% for policies issued on or after April 1, 2013).
* **Policies issued between April 1, 2003 and March 31, 2012**: Annual premium payable must NOT exceed **20% of the actual sum assured**.
* **Failure Penalty**: If premium exceeds 10% of sum assured in any year during the policy term, the entire maturity proceeds become taxable as income in the year of receipt.

### Rule C: High-Premium ULIP Threshold (Policies Issued on or after February 1, 2021)
* **Threshold**: Aggregate annual premium payable across all ULIP policies issued on or after February 1, 2021 must NOT exceed **₹2,50,000**.
* **Tax Characterization**: If aggregate annual premium exceeds ₹2.5 lakh:
  * Exemption under Schedule II(2) is withdrawn for the non-qualifying policies.
  * Proceeds on maturity or surrender are taxed as **Capital Gains** under equity/debt mutual fund taxation equivalents depending on portfolio equity allocation (equity-oriented vs non-equity ULIP).

### Rule D: High-Premium Traditional / Non-ULIP Threshold (Policies Issued on or after April 1, 2023)
* **Threshold**: Aggregate annual premium payable across non-ULIP policies issued on or after April 1, 2023 must NOT exceed **₹5,00,000**.
* **Tax Characterization**: If aggregate annual premium exceeds ₹5 lakh:
  * Exemption under Schedule II(2) is withdrawn for the non-qualifying policies.
  * Maturity proceeds less total premiums paid over the life of the policy are taxed as **Income from Other Sources** at normal applicable slab rates.
  * **Crucial Rule**: Does NOT apply to pure term insurance policies (where no maturity or survival benefit is payable).

## 3. Tax Deductions on Premium Payments: Old vs New Regime Realities

### The Simplified / New Tax Regime (Default from FY 2023–24 through FY 2026–27)
* **Deduction Status**: Deductions under former Section 80C for life insurance premiums are **NOT ELIGIBLE** under the default tax regime.
* **Practical Implication**: For the vast majority of young Indian salaried employees and tech professionals who opt for the lower tax slabs of the new regime, buying life insurance provides **zero tax deduction benefit at the time of premium payment**. The purchase decision must be based solely on pure protection value or investment merit, not false marketing of "tax savings".

### The Old Tax Regime (Optional)
* Life insurance premiums eligible up to ₹1,50,000 under former Section 80C, subject to the 10% sum-assured condition.
* Surrender within 2 years (single premium) or 2 years of regular premium payment triggers clawback of previously claimed tax deductions.
